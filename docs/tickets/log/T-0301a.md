### Rulings
**AC-10 counts as met.** The AC has two halves. The half that carries the principle — *the gate costs nothing signed out* — is measured by assertions that are not order-dependent (three spies at zero, plus the import-graph source test) and QA measured it independently. The half that is defective is the *rendering* assertion, and its failure mode in isolation is a `React.lazy` + `Suspense fallback={null}` shell producing an empty first frame — which is a property of T-0318's route wiring, not of this gate. Principle 5 is not at risk either way: `resolved` starts `true` when signed out and defaults to `true` outside a provider, so `/welcome`'s first render never waits for the gate. Rework now would change a test to match a shell this ticket does not own. **T-0331** carries it: either make the assertion order-independent or restate the AC to match the lazy shell. I would not accept a second AC in this state.

**On the process failures — accepted, with the reservation stated.** Two things happened that are independently sufficient reasons to distrust a green suite: a build reported done with three files uncommitted, one a debugging probe (`welcomeStandsDown` hardcoded to `return true`); and the suite was green while two assertions were hollow, one of them hiding a real, UI-reachable data-integrity defect (the account switch). I accept anyway, and the reason is specific rather than charitable: **every one of those defects was found inside this process, not after it** — the probe by the test suite (5 failed with it, 163/163 without), the account switch by QA against the committed build, the hollow unmount assertion by QA's own mutation test. The delivered behaviour is correct and, more importantly, the two load-bearing halves of the account-switch fix were **fault-proven separately** (reverting `run()`'s dep list fails exactly the 2 account-switch tests; disabling only the `status` reset fails exactly the 1 committed-frames test), no `it.fails` declarations remain, and the `stale`-is-gated default (D-0073 §3) now has a test where it previously had none. What I will not do is treat this as normal: the probe incident cost review and QA a stopped run each, and review's first `request-changes` was spent on a defect the build was concurrently fixing uncommitted. The process fix for that is already landed as a trap ("require a clean worktree before review or QA", commit `21c0712`); **T-0327** carries the build-lane brief. My residual doubt is about coverage *depth*, not correctness: two hollow assertions in one ticket suggests the build's own fault injection is weaker than its test count implies, and T-0332 exists because a third mutation (disabling the `/welcome/*` stand-down) still only *hangs* the suite rather than failing it.

Correct today only because `unknown` fails open; a fourth status would turn it into a trap. Whoever takes T-0328 must resolve T-0333 in the same breath.
